# Support Letters (write to documents/letters/)

4-6 letters, the MAJORITY independent (never co-authored, never same
institution); 5-8 only when each still says something new. Letters are
advisory opinions — the Policy Manual says they "should not form the
cornerstone" of the claim, and letters without documentary corroboration
are discounted. Every letter fact must be shadowed by an exhibit.

## The criteria-assignment matrix (build BEFORE inviting anyone)

A table: signer | credential | criteria assigned | unique angle. Rules:
- ≥1 letter per argued criterion; no two letters cover identical ground or
  argue the same criterion the same way.
- Match credential to criterion: journal editor → judging; independent
  researcher who cited the work → original contributions; department head
  or project director with firsthand knowledge → critical role (8 CFR
  204.5(g)(1) expects experience evidence from employers); award-committee
  member → awards.
- Signer strength ladder: independent expert > industry leader elsewhere >
  collaborator > former supervisor > current colleague (discounted as
  self-serving). ≥2 fully independent signers; an all-colleague roster is
  weak.
- ≥1 letter explicitly addresses SUSTAINED acclaim (the multi-year arc).
- Established professionals only (no students/postdocs); familiarity with
  the work beats title; U.S.-based preferred for half or more. Collect
  each signer's CV early — max 5 pages (or a bio page); it is filed
  directly behind their signed letter.

## Content bar

The letter must document what other researchers/organizations ACTUALLY DID
because of the work — adopted the method, built on the dataset, changed
practice — with names and dates. Letters asserting novelty, listing
adjectives, or speculating about future influence ("could enable therapies
...") are categorically discounted; so are letters that merely restate
USCIS criteria language in the signer's mouth. NEVER use in any letter:
speculative language about the work (potential/possible/may/probably),
qualifying language (young researcher / early-career / rising star), or
the signer's own citation/publication counts (invites unfair comparison —
keep them out of the attached CV/bio too).

## Skeleton (500-850 words each)

Vary the voice: identical phrasing across letters invites the inference
the applicant wrote them — which is exactly how these are drafted, so
rewrite each in a genuinely different register.

1. Who I am — credentials in 2-3 sentences.
2. How I know the work — independents state explicitly that they know it
   only from the published record; dependents give the specific
   relationship with dates.
3. Assigned-criterion paragraphs (2-3): the contribution in plain
   language → specific verifiable facts (what the signer's own group or
   named third parties did with it, with numbers) → why it matters in the
   field. For an independent citer: their paper, its venue, the
   applicant's paper it cited (authorship position stated), and reference
   to 1-2 OTHER notable independent uses.
4. Field comparison in the signer's own experience ("In my N years, few
   researchers have ...") — concrete, not superlative-stacked.
5. Summary opinion mapping to the standard in natural words (top of the
   field, recognition sustained over time) + offer of further information.

All letters: letterhead + date placeholders on top; signature block
requiring hand/verified signature ("do not simply type your signature") +
contact info (USCIS occasionally verifies). Every fact must match
case.json exactly; refresh counts at signature time.
