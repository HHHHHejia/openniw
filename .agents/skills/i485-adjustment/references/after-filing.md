# Stage V — After Filing

Write `timeline.md` the day the package ships and keep it current. Everything below is a range
or an expectation, never a promise: USCIS commits to none of it, and times heard from friends
or forums are unreliable.

**Never hardcode the EAD and advance-parole windows.** Read the posted times for **I-765
category (c)(9)** and **I-131** at `egov.uscis.gov/processing-times` when timeline.md is
written, record the figure with the date checked, and re-read it each session — they move,
and 2026 reports of C09 processing run materially longer than the 4-8 months older
firm-experience guidance quotes. A user planning around a stale window may stop working
before the card arrives.

## Receipts and biometrics

- Proofread every notice on arrival: name spelling, **name order**, date of birth, address,
  and the receipt date. Errors here become card-printing errors later.
- A lockbox filing may produce an I-797 with **no A-Number**; USCIS sends it in a second
  notice a few days later. That is normal, not an error.
- One receipt per form per person — a couple filing I-485 + I-765 + I-131 each gets six. Nothing
  by 30 days → escalate; no biometrics notice by 90 days → service request.
- **Biometrics**: bring all ASC notices plus unexpired government photo ID; nothing else is
  needed. Duplicate notices are normal; children under 14 usually get none. At the appointment
  the applicant signs an oath **re-affirming the application's contents** — so anything that
  changed since filing matters. Missing the appointment can mean denial. **Keep the stamped
  notice** as proof of attendance. Rescheduling in advance is allowed and does not harm the
  I-485, but the next slot is unpredictable.
- The **background check has no timeline and cannot be expedited**; service requests do not help.
  Say so plainly rather than letting the user chase it.

## EAD and advance parole

- Timing keys off the **receipt date**, not the biometrics date, and is independent of
  priority-date currency; receiving the card years before the green card is normal. Expedites run
  only on published USCIS criteria — wanting to work is explicitly not one, and a request made
  before biometrics may be refused for that reason alone.
- **(c)(9) EAD validity is now 18 months**, initial and renewal, and **the automatic extension on
  timely renewal is gone** for renewals filed on or after 2025-10-30 — work authorization ends the
  day the card expires, with no bridge. This is the single most dangerous stale belief a user may
  carry in from older guidance.
- Renewal window: **not earlier than 180 days before expiry** (earlier filings may be
  rejected), and the applicant must be **physically in the U.S.** when the renewal is filed.
  Plan backwards from the hard expiry date; nobody sends a reminder.
- Renewal contents per person: the form, two new photos never previously submitted, the fee
  authorization, a copy of the I-485 receipt notice, colour front-and-back copies of **every**
  EAD ever issued, a colour copy of one photo ID (preferably expiring more than a year out), and
  — for an I-131 renewal — the most recent I-94 record and status document, the current card,
  and any 212(e) documentation.
- **Combo card check**: a single card serves as both only if it carries the advance-parole legend
  near the bottom; without it, it is an EAD and does not authorize re-entry. If the I-485 is
  approved while a renewal is pending, that renewal will likely be denied — normal and harmless;
  if one is mistakenly approved anyway, do not use it.

## Travel while the I-485 is pending

- **Departure without advance parole abandons the I-485** — unless the applicant fits the
  narrow exception for someone in lawful **H-1/H-4 or L-1/L-2** (and K or V) status who
  remains eligible for it, is returning to resume work for the same employer where that
  applies, and holds a **valid H/L visa foil**.
- **Advance parole must be approved and in hand before departure.** A pending I-131 is not
  travel authorization, and **departing while the I-131 is pending kills that application**.
- Re-entering on advance parole means being **paroled**, not admitted — the underlying
  nonimmigrant status ends. Re-entering on the H/L visa preserves it. That fork is the hard
  stop below, not a recommendation this skill makes.
- An advance parole document does not entitle anyone to be paroled: a CBP officer makes a
  fresh discretionary decision at the port of entry, and DHS may revoke the document at any
  time, including while the traveller is abroad.
- Travelling on a current advance parole while a renewal is pending: **return before the
  current document expires**. A new biometrics appointment can be triggered mid-trip with no
  warning.
- Renewing an existing H or L visa stamp abroad does not harm a pending I-485. **Using a
  consulate to START a status** (rather than renew) risks abandoning it — that change must
  happen inside the U.S.
- Practitioners advise keeping total time outside the U.S. modest while the case is pending, on
  the theory that adjustment is meant to happen from inside the country. Practice, not a rule.

## Interview or waiver

The regulation has always required an interview for every adjustment applicant unless USCIS
waives it, and the waiver is discretionary and case by case. Employment-based applicants have
never been on the published waiver list — they are waived, when they are, under a catch-all.
No published rule changed in 2026; what varies is operational practice, so state no rate and
no direction of travel. **Correct posture: assume an interview, prepare for it, and treat a
waiver as a bonus. A waiver cannot be requested.** Do not quote circulating waiver-rate
percentages; none of them traces to a source.

Published reasons a case gets sent to a field office include: confirming identity; validating
status; unresolved manner-of-entry questions; criminal or national-security concerns; fraud
concerns; fingerprints rejected twice; a Class A medical condition the service center cannot
resolve by RFE; a missing file; and **a "Yes" to any eligibility question the service center
cannot resolve by RFE** — the mechanical link back to Part 9.

Preparation is clerical and belongs here: assemble an **originals file** — everything filed,
in original where one exists, including expired passports and old I-94s — plus the complete
package copy. At the interview the officer verifies the applicant understood the questions and
offers a chance to correct or update answers; if anything is added or revised, the applicant
re-signs and dates the form there. An interpreter may be brought and completes an oath and
privacy release. Derivatives appear too.

## While the case sits

- **Retrogression after filing does not unmake the filing.** A visa must be available both at
  filing and at final adjudication, so a retrogressed case is held rather than denied, and the
  priority date and place in line are unaffected. The pending-case benefits — work
  authorization, advance parole, the 180-day portability mark, and a period of stay authorized
  — continue. Long-delayed cases do draw second-look RFEs and refreshed background checks, so
  keep the address current and keep records.
- **There is no channel to supplement a pending I-485** other than answering an RFE. New
  evidence is collected and held until asked for. Keep collecting continued-engagement
  evidence if the principal was unemployed at filing or becomes so.
- **Job change or layoff → hard stop.** Whether new work is in the "same or similar
  occupational classification" is a totality-of-the-circumstances legal judgment under INA
  204(j), and a Supplement J that gets it wrong is a self-inflicted denial. Self-petitioners
  face a **different** question with no bright line and no form to file: whether they are
  still working in the area or field that formed the basis of their eligibility. Collect the
  facts — old and new job titles, duties, SOC codes, wages, employer — and route the
  determination to counsel. Note only the mechanical fact that the 180-day mark runs from the
  I-485 receipt date to the date USCIS receives a porting request.

## If an RFE arrives

Read the sibling skills' R workflow (`niw-petition` or `eb1a-petition`,
`references/rfe-response.md`) if either is installed; reuse its mechanics either way.
Timeliness is RECEIVED-BY, not postmark; the printed deadline controls and cannot be extended;
you respond **once**, with everything, because a partial response is a request for a decision on
the record as it stands; and the response goes to the address on the notice, never to a lockbox.
Every I-485 hard stop stays in force — an RFE touching Part 9, status history, 245(k), or
portability goes to an attorney, not into a drafted answer.

## The decision

- **Approval**: permanent residence is recorded as of the date of the approving order; the card
  follows, typically within weeks. **Do not travel before the physical card arrives**, even
  holding EAD and advance parole. If a trip cannot wait, a temporary evidence-of-residence stamp
  can be requested at a field office with a passport, the approval notice and proof of the need
  to travel — issuance is discretionary, so the fallback is to move the trip. If the applicant is
  abroad when approval issues, they return on the EAD/AP with the approval notice and passport
  and explain at the port of entry. The address-change duty continues for as long as they are a
  permanent resident.
- **Denial → hard stop, immediately.** There is no appeal from an I-485 denial. A motion to
  reopen or reconsider runs on a short clock measured from the date USCIS mailed the decision,
  does not stay anything, does not revive the EAD or advance parole, and does not stop
  unlawful presence from accruing. A denial also ends every derivative's case at the same
  moment. If the applicant is not lawfully present when the denial issues, current USCIS
  policy is that a Notice to Appear **will** issue. Motion versus re-file versus depart is
  attorney work with a fuse measured in days — say that, give the user the record they need
  (the denial notice, which must set out the positive and negative factors where discretion
  was the ground), and stop.
- **Approval is not a terminal state** in the current environment: USCIS has stood up
  centralized vetting that reviews approved cases as well as pending ones. Say it once,
  factually, and do not speculate about it.
