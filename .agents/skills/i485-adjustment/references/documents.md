# Stage II·b — The Document Checklist

Build `documents/checklist.md` from the drivers below, personalized to what this
family actually is. Each line: item · who it is for · why it is required · status
(`have` / `requested` / `blocked`) · where it sits in `sources/`. Export it with
`openniw docx`. Re-verify the required-evidence list against uscis.gov/i-485Checklist
before you finalize.

## Driver 1 — always, for every applicant (principal and each derivative)

- Signed **Form I-485**, current edition, printed with its per-page barcode.
- **Form I-693 medical**, one per applicant, handled per the channel (below).
- Colour copy of **one current, unexpired government photo ID** — passport photo page,
  or a driver's licence / state ID (if a licence: **front and back**).
- Colour copies of **passport pages**: photo/bio page plus every U.S. visa stamp,
  entry stamp and immigration endorsement, including expired ones and ones in expired
  passports. Foreign visas and blank pages are not wanted.
- **I-94, front and back**, most recent entry — the CBP/passport I-94, not the
  tear-off at the bottom of an H or O approval notice.
- **Birth document** (Driver 3 — the single largest RFE generator).
- **Photographs**: 2 per application that person files (I-485 + I-765 + I-131 = 6 per
  person; I-485 + I-765 = 4). Within the last 30 days, never previously submitted,
  plain white or off-white background, 2×2 trimmed, head 1 to 1⅜ inches, name lightly
  on the back in pencil or felt pen, not stacked while wet.
- **Fee authorization**, one payment instrument per application per person.
- Copy of the I-140 receipt or approval **notice** (unless filing concurrently).

## Driver 2 — status history

Everything in `references/history.md`: every I-20, DS-2019 and I-797, colour copies
(front and back) of every EAD ever held, and the signed explanations for anything that
could not be produced. Grouped by person.

## Driver 3 — country of birth

### The acceptance test

A birth certificate is adequate only if it shows **all five**: the applicant's name;
date of birth; place of birth; **at least one** parent's name; and an indication that
the birth was **registered with the civil authority having jurisdiction over births in
that locality**. Two consequences cause most failures: **consulate-issued and
hospital-issued certificates are generally not accepted on their own** (the issuer must
be the municipality or state of birth), and **country of birth governs, not
citizenship** — someone born in country A who naturalized in B still needs an A birth
document. Check the DOS **Reciprocity and Civil Documents by Country** schedule for
what that country issues, and send the user there too.

### The substitute-evidence bundle — CUMULATIVE, not alternatives

When the primary document is missing or deficient, all three parts are filed together:

```
   the deficient or absent primary document — or a Non-Availability of Birth
   Certificate (NABC) standing in its place
 + TWO affidavits from older relatives who knew of the birth (ideally both
   parents; otherwise an older relative, preferably present at the birth;
   each affiant over 18 at the time of the birth)
 + SECONDARY evidence naming the applicant, date of birth and parent(s),
   preferably issued when the applicant was young
```

**Affidavit content you draft** (own wording, applicant's facts): affiant's identity
and relationship to the applicant; how long they have known the parents; the
applicant's date, city and country of birth and the parents' names; the **specific
defect** being explained (no name on the record / registered late / never registered);
for a non-parent affiant, a statement of presence at or direct knowledge of the birth;
an attestation of truthfulness. Each is sworn before a notary and carries the notary
stamp. Colour copies may be filed; keep originals for the interview. **Secondary
evidence, best first**: a secondary-school certificate (strong even if it lists only
name and DOB); the parentage page of a passport; religious ceremony records; other
early government records. A record naming only one parent is fine.

### The three deficiency branches

| Branch | Trigger | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| **No name on the record** | registered without the child's given name | first try to have the civil authority reissue a corrected certificate showing the name; only if that fails, the three-part bundle |
| **Late registration** | birth registered roughly a year or more after the birth | three-part bundle. The **registration** date matters, not the issue date — a recently *issued* certificate showing contemporaneous registration needs nothing extra |
| **No certificate exists** | no record or registry | three-part bundle with an **NABC** in the primary slot |

**NABC requirements**: issued by the local or municipal government of the place of
birth (not a consulate), on official letterhead or the government's own form, signed
by the official with custody of the birth records, stating that a search of the birth
registration records for the stated year(s) was made at the applicant's request and no
record was found. Where no standard form exists, a locally signed and sealed letter in
that shape is used; if the official refuses, fall back to a late-registration
certificate. **Partial names** can suffice where the name is part of the maiden name,
or the full current name is deducible from the document as a whole.

### Country specifics

| Country of birth | What is wanted |
|---|---|
| **Mainland China** | the **notarial birth certificate** (出生公证书) — required even after naturalizing elsewhere. Typically a booklet: Chinese detail page, its English translation, a Chinese page certifying the translation, and that page's translation; watermark, seal and red stamp. Colour copies accepted |
| **Taiwan** | **two** documents — an individual Household Registration Transcript (個人戶籍謄本) with **all remarks included**, and a hospital-issued birth record obtainable via the household registration office. Bilingual and Chinese-only versions both circulate; submit a certified translation with a Chinese-only original, or file both versions |
| **South Korea** | **both** the detailed Basic Certificate (기본증명서(상세)) and the detailed Family Relations Certificate (가족관계증명서(상세)), from a Korean local government office or the courts' online service — **not** from an embassy or consulate abroad. Full English translation |
| **Turkey** | **Nüfus Kayıt Örneği** (extract of vital record) via e-Devlet, showing the applicant and both parents (and spouse and children if married). Each applicant supplies their own, as "self". Full English translation |
| **Nepal / Pakistan** | Nepal: Citizenship Card plus translation, as secondary evidence. Pakistan: NADRA Family Registration Certificate, which maps every family relationship in one document |
| **Born where the applicant is not a national and cannot obtain the document** | show the disability, then file secondary evidence (hospital records from the place of birth, alternate or unofficial versions, an embassy-issued birth document as secondary only) **plus** a signed, currently dated letter giving full name, DOB, city and country of birth, country of citizenship, which documents are offered as birth evidence, why the preferred document cannot be obtained, and an attestation that the birth details are accurate. Volume helps; sufficiency is the officer's call |

## Driver 4 — marital status

Spouse filing: one colour copy of the marriage certificate issued by the government
agency where the marriage was **registered** — the jurisdiction where the ceremony
physically occurred, not a later home-country consular registration (high RFE risk
unless the ceremony took place inside the consulate). Any prior divorce: the official
decree from the government that processed it. Non-English: copy plus translation.

Married under two years at filing: bona fide marriage evidence is advisable. Strongest
first — children's birth certificates; joint tax returns; insurance naming the spouse
as beneficiary; deeds or leases naming both; joint bank and credit accounts (keep the
earliest **and** latest statements, to show duration). Family affidavits and
photographs are weak; hold them unless there is nothing else, and caption any
photograph with date, place, everyone pictured and their relationship to the couple.

## Driver 5 — children

For each child filing, that child's birth certificate showing the applicant as parent
(plus certified translation). Children under 14 filing with a parent's I-485 pay a
reduced fee and usually receive no biometrics notice — one arriving anyway is normal.
Signatures: 14 or older signs their own forms; for a younger child a parent may sign,
annotating the signature to show they signed as parent.

## Driver 6 — employment (principal only; never required of derivatives)

| Situation | Required |
|---|---|
| Currently employed | employment verification letter (content in `forms.md`) **plus** the last 3 months of salary history — pay stubs, or bank statements showing salary deposits |
| PhD candidate on a stipend | the student-flavoured letter variant (`forms.md`) |
| No income but spouse employed | the spouse's employment letter and salary history instead; nothing further |
| Neither employed | self-support evidence: recent bank statements, U.S. property deeds, vehicle title, retirement and investment accounts, securities |
| Unemployed at filing | self-support **and**, separately, continued-engagement evidence in the same occupational field — offer letters, job applications and interview correspondence, peer-review invitations, papers submitted or in draft, conference participation. Keep collecting after filing |
| Employer-petitioned I-140 (EB-1B, EB-2 PERM, EB-3) | Form I-485 Supplement J, per `forms.md` |
| Self-petitioned I-140 (EB-1A, NIW) | **no Supplement J** — a signed statement of continuing intent instead, and not even that when filing concurrently with the I-140 |

## The medical exam (Form I-693)

- Must be submitted **with** the I-485; filing without it risks rejection. Find a civil surgeon
  via the USCIS locator; fees vary and are the applicant's.
- The applicant fills **only Part 1 and the name/A-Number header on each page**, and
  **does not sign or date until the civil surgeon says to** (in their presence).
- **Edition rule is unusual**: the acceptable edition is the one in force when the
  **civil surgeon signs**. Check uscis.gov/i-693 before the appointment.
- **Paper filing: the sealed envelope goes into the package unopened.** Do not accept
  the form unless sealed; USCIS returns an I-693 whose envelope is open or altered.
  Ask the surgeon for a copy for the applicant's records.
- **Online filing inverts this**: open the envelope, upload the I-693, and retain the
  original form and envelope until a final decision — USCIS may ask for them.
- **90-day warning**: anyone who will leave the U.S. and return on a status requiring
  nonimmigrant intent (F, J, B and similar), or who is extending or changing into one,
  should not complete the medical until back in the U.S. for at least 90 days.
- A wrong or missing A-Number on a sealed I-693 is **not** worth breaking the seal over.
  Latent TB with a clear chest x-ray is not itself a bar; if an x-ray was taken, confirm
  the radiologist's signed report is inside the envelope.
- **Validity** (civil-surgeon signature dated **on or after 2023-11-01**): scoped to **the
  application it was filed with**, for as long as that application is pending. A signature dated
  before 2023-11-01 retains evidentiary value for two years from signature. The 01/20/25 I-693
  instructions still print a superseded "valid for two years" sentence — do not repeat it to the
  user. USCIS may ask for an updated exam at any time.
- Two different events, opposite answers. **Rejection** — the application was never properly
  filed: a package returned with the envelope opened may be **refiled using that same I-693**,
  together with the enclosures the civil surgeon put in and a copy of the rejection notice.
  **Withdrawal or denial** — a **new I-693 is required** for any future filing; the old one is
  not portable.

## Translations

Any foreign-language document is filed as a copy of the original **plus** a certified
English translation. The applicant may not translate their own documents and neither
may a family member; a friend, colleague or service fluent in both languages is fine.
The translation must be word-for-word and should mirror the original's layout, so the
officer can see nothing was skipped. The certification carries at minimum the
translator's typed name, signature, address and date, plus a statement that they are
competent in both languages and that the translation of the named document is
accurate. Copies of translations are fine; a Latin diploma can be handled by printing
the university's own published English rendering with the URL visible.
