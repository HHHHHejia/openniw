# Stage IV — Assembly, Fees, Payment, Mailing

Nothing gets printed until the completeness pass passes. Write the result into
`package/assembly.md` and the mailing plan into `package/mailing.md`.

## The completeness pass (run over the whole case folder, per person)

1. **Gates** — eligibility.md still true for the month the package will be RECEIVED? Re-run the
   Visa Bulletin lookup; a chart that turned over invalidates the filing.
2. **Editions** — every form the current edition on its own USCIS page today; every page footer
   showing the same edition and a page number. **Check the postmark against 2026-09-18**: a
   package postmarked (or e-submitted) on or after that date needs the revised I-485 edition and
   is adjudicated under the restored discretionary public-charge test; before it, the 2022 rule
   and the 01/20/25 edition. State which side the package lands on; do not advise on timing it.
3. **Blanks** — every item answered, `N/A` vs `None` used correctly, every signature present and
   in ink, every extra sheet signed and dated.
4. **Consistency** — names, DOB, A-Number, I-94 number, passport number, addresses and dates
   identical across every form and letter, and identical to case.json. This is the single most
   common self-filer defect.
5. **Documents** — checklist.md fully `have`, one sealed I-693 per applicant, every translation
   certified, every gap covered by a document or a signed explanation.
6. **Fees** — recomputed today from the current fee schedule; one payment authorization per form
   per person; amounts exact.
7. **Address** — pulled live today from the USCIS I-485 direct-filing-addresses page, with the
   `Attn:` line copied verbatim and the right variant for the carrier.
8. **Hard stops** — no unresolved hard stop is blocking any part of the package. If one is, the
   package does not ship; say so plainly.

## Printing

- **Single-sided only**, 8½ × 11. Nothing double-sided. Sign in **black ink**.
- **Do not shrink** when copying — EAD cards, driver's licences and passport pages are the usual
  offenders. Avoid oversized documents.
- **Colour copies wherever colour carries information**: photo IDs, passport pages and visa
  stamps, EAD cards (front and back), birth and marriage certificates. A black-and-white copy of
  an old EAD is a last resort when no colour copy exists.
- Check the per-page barcode on every printed page of the I-485, I-765 and I-131.

## Fastening and tabs

**No staples, no three-hole punch, no binders or folders that cannot be taken apart.** Use paper
clips, binder clips or ACCO fasteners, grouped **by application and by person**. Tabs to help
the officer find exhibits are allowed as long as they do not impede disassembly.

## What attaches to what — per person, per form

A photo-ID copy is clipped to **each form each person files**, not once per package:

| Behind | Attach |
|---|---|
| each person's **I-485** | colour copy of one current photo ID |
| each person's **I-765** | colour copy of one photo ID **plus** front and back colour copies of **every** EAD ever held |
| each person's **I-131** | colour copy of one photo ID **plus** the most recent I-94 record, the most recent status document, and any 212(e) evidence |
| **Supplement J** (only where required) | colour copy of one photo ID |
| top of the form it pays for | the payment authorization |
| front of the form it relates to | **G-1145**, optional, one per applicant |

The **sealed I-693 envelope** goes into the package **intact and unopened**, one per applicant,
labelled outside so the intake clerk can tell whose is whose. **Online filers do the opposite**:
open the envelope, upload the I-693, and keep the original form and envelope until a decision.

## Originals vs copies

Submit copies, not originals, unless a document is specifically required in original form —
USCIS does not return materials, and originals are what the applicant hands the officer at an
interview. The employment verification letter is the standard exception: send the ink-signed
original if obtainable, a high-quality colour copy if not.

## Assembly order (top to bottom, per applicant bundle)

payment authorization → G-1145 (if used) → cover sheet → cover letter → signed I-485 →
Supplement J or the self-petitioner intent statement → I-693 sealed envelope → I-765 with its
attachments → I-131 with its attachments → I-140 receipt or approval notice → identity and
status documents (photo ID, passport pages, I-94, all I-797s, I-20s, DS-2019s, EADs) → birth
document and any substitute bundle → marriage or divorce documents → employment letter and
salary history → any remaining supporting evidence, each explanation attachment filed **behind
the exhibit it explains**.

Principal's bundle first, then each derivative's, clipped separately.

## Cover sheet and cover letter

**Cover sheet**, on the very front: applicant name, the I-140 receipt number, the A-Number if
one exists, and an inventory of what is enclosed.

**Cover letter**, one page: the lockbox address block; a RE line naming the filing and the
preference category with its statutory cite; a roster naming the principal applicant and each
derivative; then, briefly — that adjustment is sought under INA 245 on the identified I-140 ·
for the principal and each derivative separately, the current nonimmigrant status with its
expiry, the visa class and date of last entry · that the priority date is current on the chart
USCIS designated for this month, with each applicant's country of birth · for a self-petition,
that no Supplement J is required because the category carries no job offer · any 212(e)
disposition, cross-referenced to the attached proof · a numbered ENCLOSED DOCUMENTS list in
physical package order · signature block with contact details.

**Cross-chargeability (INA 202(b)(2)) — condition on it.** The paragraph goes in **only** where
both spouses are independently eligible to adjust, both I-485s are in this same family pack, and
the spouse's birth certificate (certified translation) and marriage certificate are attached; a
Gate 2 chart answer that depends on the claim is only true under those conditions, and a rejected
filing loses its filing date. Uncertainty about the marriage's bona fides or timing, or about the
spouse's own eligibility to adjust, is a hard stop (`eligibility.md` Gate 2) — then the letter
states each applicant's country of birth and nothing more. When it does go in, do not bury it:
name the principal's country of birth, the spouse's, and the request, in one sentence.

**Unique circumstances are explained in an attachment filed behind the relevant exhibit, never
in the cover letter.** The cover letter states the filing; explanations live with the documents
they explain.

## Fees — recompute from the live schedule, never from this page

Verified 2026-08-04 against USCIS Form G-1055 (edition 05/29/26). **Re-read uscis.gov/g-1055 at
assembly time**; an incorrect fee is a rejection, and Pub. L. 119-21 fees adjust annually.

| Form | Paper | Online |
|---|---|---|
| I-485, applicant over 14 | $1,440 | $1,390 |
| I-485, under 14 filed **concurrently with a parent's** I-485 | $950 | $900 |
| I-765, where the I-485 was filed with a fee on/after 2024-04-01 and is still pending | $260 | $260 |
| I-131 advance parole on a pending I-485 | $630 | $580 |
| I-693 · Supplement J · G-1145 | $0 | $0 |
| I-485 Supplement A (245(i) — out of scope) | $1,000 | $1,000 |

A typical paper package for one adult principal filing I-485 + I-765 + I-131 is $1,440 + $260 +
$630 = **$2,330**, as **three separate payments**. **Biometrics is included** — there is no
separate biometrics fee for an I-485; do not let a keyword match on "biometric" in the fee
schedule produce a phantom charge. Note also that an advance-parole approval sits inside a fee
block carrying the Pub. L. 119-21 parole fee, whose statutory exception appears to cover an
adjustment applicant returning from temporary travel — but the exception is applied by USCIS or
CBP, not claimed by the traveller. Surface it as an open question; do not promise $0.

## Payment mechanics

**Since 2025-10-28, USCIS accepts only electronic payment for paper-filed forms** (EO 14247;
predecessor news release 2025-08-29) — checks, money orders, cashier's cheques and cash are out.
Guidance printed before that date describes the old regime. What is accepted:

- **Form G-1450** — credit, debit or prepaid card (U.S.-issued).
- **Form G-1650** — ACH debit from a **U.S.** bank account; foreign banks cannot be used.
- Online filing pays by card or bank withdrawal inside the USCIS account.
- **Form G-1651** — the paper-payment exemption, for an applicant who genuinely cannot pay
  electronically (no access to banking or electronic payment, undue hardship, or another
  recognized ground). The paper instrument travels with the G-1651.

Rules that cause rejections: **one payment method per benefit request** — never a G-1450 and a
G-1650 in the same package, and never a paper instrument combined with anything else · **one
payment authorization per form per person**; a single combined payment for several forms can get
the **entire package** rejected · the authorized amount must be exact · on a G-1650,
business-vs-personal and checking-vs-savings must both be marked and the routing and account
numbers must be right · a declined card or a failed ACH is a rejection for lack of payment · a
card has a daily transaction limit, so a large total may need splitting across two G-1450s for
the *same* form.

## Where to mail — teach the lookup, never a cached table

```
1. Open the USCIS "Direct Filing Addresses for Form I-485" page today.
2. Filing concurrently with the I-140?
     YES → use the "Employment-Based Categories Filing Form I-485 Concurrently with
           Form I-140" section (not state-based). BUT if I-907 premium processing is
           also requested for the I-140, that section sends the whole bundle to the
           address in the FORM I-140 filing instructions instead — a different page.
     NO  → use "Certain Special Immigrant Employment-Based Categories and
           Employment-Based Categories with a Pending or Approved Form I-140".
           This section IS state-based: find the applicant's state of residence.
3. Read BOTH addresses in the cell — the USPS P.O. Box and the separate street
   address for FedEx/UPS/DHL. They are different places. Choose by carrier.
4. Copy the "Attn:" line verbatim.
5. Re-fetch and re-verify immediately before mailing.
```

Lockbox assignments are reshuffled without a form revision and there is no inferable geographic
logic — never infer, never cache. Mailing to the wrong location can get the filing rejected, and
**a rejected filing does not keep its filing date**.

Mailing rules: file related forms in one envelope (I-485 with its I-765 and I-131 is exactly the
intended case) · FedEx must be "Direct Signature Required" — lockboxes reject "Adult Signature
Required" · use a trackable service and keep proof of the delivery date, because **the month
USCIS RECEIVES the package is the month whose visa chart applies** · never send an RFE response,
an appeal or forgotten evidence to a lockbox · **keep a complete copy of everything filed**,
which is also the record the applicant brings to an interview.
