# National-Importance Source Research (Prong 1 & PES §2 policy hooks)

How to find, verify, and rank the government sources behind every "policy
hook → my contribution" pair. Run this before drafting pes.md §2 and the
Petition Letter Prong-1 modules. Save the results (used AND not-used) as
`citations/policy-hooks.md` with a "SOURCES NOT USED" section, so later
sessions never re-run dead-end searches. Archive every page consulted under
`sources/fetched/` (dated) — captures are exhibit material.

## MANDATORY currency rule — verify before citing

Executive orders and national strategies change with administrations: the
Oct-2023 AI EO 14110 was RESCINDED Jan 2025 (replaced by EO 14179); IRA
programs were partially repealed/modified in 2025. Citing a dead authority
is a credibility hit with the officer. Before citing ANY EO, strategy, or
initiative, confirm it is still in force on whitehouse.gov /
federalregister.gov and archive the check. Prefer statutes and agency data
— they do not vanish by proclamation — over executive orders.

Tier 2 = national plans/statutes/EOs (currency rule applies with force).
Tier 3 = GAO / National Academies / peer-reviewed problem quantification.

never the primary evidence.

## Search-query patterns

`site:whitehouse.gov [field] executive order` · `[field] "national action
plan" site:gov` · `[field] federal investment site:nsf.gov OR site:nih.gov
OR site:energy.gov` · `[problem] GAO report` · `[problem] National
Academies report` · `[problem] "billions" OR "trillions" cost United States`

## Per-source record and the connection statement

For each candidate record: exact statistic · exact quote (copy-paste —
paraphrasing weakens the evidence) · publication title + date · URL ·
publishing agency · retrieval date. Then draft the pair as:

```
SOURCE: [government report/statistic]
QUOTE: "[exact quote]"
CONNECTION: The petitioner's work in [specific endeavor] directly addresses
this by [how]. Specifically, [project/product] [does what] for [whom],
which aligns with [government initiative/goal].
```

## Ranking the final 5-10 (in priority order)

1. **Directness** — straight line from the source's problem to the
   petitioner's SPECIFIC work (not the field);
2. **Authority** — statute/agency data > executive order > agency report >
   industry study;
3. **Recency** — ≤3 years preferred; a 2015 statistic is weaker than a
   2024 one;
4. **Quotability** — hard numbers > vague policy language.
Gold-standard test: government says X is a problem → the petitioner's work
directly addresses X → here is the evidence of that connection. Pair one
problem-statistic source with one policy/funding source per pillar.
