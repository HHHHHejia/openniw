# Support Letters (write to documents/letters/)

At most 4 letters total — fewer is often better; too many distract from
objective evidence. Ranked effectiveness:
1. **National Importance testimonial** from U.S. governmental/quasi-governmental
   entities (incl. state universities, national labs, USPS-class bodies)
2. **Planned Research testimonial** from employers/supervisors/advisors/
   collaborators/funding-org representatives
3. **Recommendation letters** on completed research — dependent (first-hand)
   preferred; independents must have cited the work

Signer selection: established professionals only (no students/postdocs);
familiarity with the work beats title; ≥half U.S.-based, ideally different
states. The signer's CV/bio for the exhibit must NOT contain their own
citation/publication counts (invites unfair comparison). Collect each
signer's CV early — max 5 pages (a printed bio page works if no CV); it is
filed directly behind their signed letter in the package.

NEVER use in any letter: speculative language about the work (potential/
possible/may/probably), qualifying language (young researcher / early-career /
rising star), or the signer's own metrics.

## Templates (500–850 words each; vary voice between letters)

**National Importance TL** ("Dear USCIS,"): signer credentials + relationship
+ thesis → lay explanation of the research area and any joint project →
funding-as-endorsement paragraph if the signer's org funds the work →
national statistics with a consequence chain (statistic → bridge sentence →
claim) → conclusion naming 3 specific technical competencies →
professional-opinion recommendation → offer of further information.

**Planned Research TL** ("To the Adjudicating Officer,"): capacity +
relationship + endeavor restated VERBATIM + "Please accept this letter as
verification of [the] pursuit of [the] proposed endeavor and our ongoing
research project." → project paragraph (what / builds on / how it supports
the endeavor / where performed / funding source / national issues) →
national-issue paragraph (gap → harm → what the work introduces →
second-order benefits) → conclusion.

**Independent recommendation** (5 paragraphs): credentials + explicit
independence statement (knows the work only from the published record) →
the citing act (their paper, venue, the applicant's paper it cited, the
applicant's authorship position stated explicitly) → plain-language
explanation of the cited contribution + reference to 1-2 OTHER notable
citations by third parties → field-and-nation link → "On the basis of the
published record alone, [name]'s continued work in the United States serves
the national interest."

**Dependent recommendation** (6-7 paragraphs): credentials + specific
relationship with dates → context of shared work (e.g. the writer's role on
named funded projects) → SPECIFIC technical contributions attributed to the
applicant (conceived X, designed Y — quality of contribution, never
share-of-output ratios) → second project → cross-theme paragraph with
citation counts as of signing date → originality + national interest.

All letters: letterhead + date placeholders on top; signature block requiring
hand/verified signature ("do not simply type your signature") + contact info.
Every fact must match case.json exactly; refresh counts at signature time.
Academic letters do not mention the applicant's company unless that is the
letter's assigned angle.
