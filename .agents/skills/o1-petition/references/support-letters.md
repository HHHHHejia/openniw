# Expert Letters (write to documents/letters/)

These are RECOMMENDATION letters corroborating specific criteria — a
different document from the consultation/advisory opinion (drafting.md),
even when both are signed by field experts. Never have the consultation
signer also write a recommendation letter.

Practitioner norm for research-driven O-1As is 4-8 letters, at least half
from INDEPENDENT experts (know the work only from the published record);
fewer strong letters beat many weak ones — letters only corroborate, the
objective exhibits carry the criteria. Signer selection: established
professionals only (no students/postdocs); familiarity with the work
beats title; U.S.-based preferred. Collect each signer's CV early — max
5 pages (a bio page works); it is filed directly behind their signed
letter. The signer's CV must NOT contain their own citation/publication
counts (invites unfair comparison).

## Assignment matrix (build before drafting)

Assign each letter 1-2 criteria to carry, matched to what the signer can
credibly attest:
- independent researcher who cited the work → original contributions (5),
  scholarly articles (6)
- editor / program chair / grant-panel manager → judging (4)
- current or former supervisor / PI / director → critical capacity (7),
  with org chart and first-hand specifics
- investor or board member (founder cases) → critical capacity (7),
  distinguished reputation of the company, remuneration context (8) —
  as factual attestation, not expert opinion
- award/fellowship selection insider → awards (1), membership (2)
Coverage rule: every argued criterion gets ≥1 letter; no two letters
argue the same thing the same way.

## Anatomy (500-850 words; vary voice between letters)

1. Who I am — credentials, in facts.
2. How I know the Beneficiary's work — for independents, an explicit
   independence statement (never met; knows the work from the published
   record), then the citing act: their paper, venue, which paper of the
   Beneficiary's it used, the Beneficiary's authorship position stated
   explicitly.
3. Two to three substantive paragraphs on the assigned criteria — the
   criterion in natural language (NEVER the regulation's phrasing in the
   signer's mouth — template language reads as fake to adjudicators),
   specific verifiable facts, why the contribution matters, reference to
   1-2 OTHER notable uses by third parties.
4. Comparison to the field, first-person and concrete: "In my N years in
   [field], I have encountered few researchers who ..." — calibrated to
   what the signer would actually say.
5. Summary opinion tying the Beneficiary's continued U.S. work to the
   field, + offer of further information.

All letters: letterhead + date placeholders on top; signature block
requiring a hand/verified signature ("do not simply type your signature")
+ contact info (USCIS occasionally verifies). NEVER use speculative
language about the work (potential/possible/may), qualifying language
(young researcher/early-career/rising star), or the signer's own metrics.
Every fact must match case.json exactly; refresh counts at signature time.

Sources: expert-letter assignment/coverage patterns adapted from
juntoku9/claude_immigration_attorney (MIT); independence and signer
norms: gojumpstart.com, deel.com; USCIS Policy Manual Vol. 2 Part M Ch. 7
(affidavit requirements).
