Otherwise, classic recommendation letter — type matters:

INDEPENDENT recommender (never collaborated; knows the work only from the
published record). 5-paragraph architecture:
1. Credentials + independence: writer's position and research area, how they
   encountered the applicant's work through their own research; explicit
   statement of no prior collaboration or affiliation.
2. The citing act: name the writer's own paper and venue and the applicant's
   paper it cited; state the applicant's authorship position (co-first etc.)
   explicitly.
3. Plain-language explanation of the cited contribution and its recognition;
   ALSO reference at least 1–2 OTHER notable citations of the applicant's work
   by third parties (shows influence beyond the writer's own use).
4. Field-and-nation link: why this class of work matters to U.S. priorities.
5. Endorsement: "On the basis of the published record alone, [name]'s continued
   work in the United States serves the national interest."

DEPENDENT recommender (advisor, supervisor, collaborator). 6–7 paragraphs:
1. Credentials + the specific relationship with dates.
2. Context of the shared work (e.g., the writer's role on named funded projects).
3. Contribution paragraph: attribute SPECIFIC technical contributions to the
   applicant (conceived X, designed Y, first-authored Z) — quality of
   contribution, never share-of-output ratios.
4. Second project/theme paragraph.
5. Cross-theme paragraph if the writer can speak to newer work (citation counts
   as of signing date).
6. Originality + national interest: "combines genuine originality and technical
   depth... a demonstrated ability to produce work that others build upon";
   endeavor's alignment with U.S. national interests.
